## Addendum — re-verification after changing the edge default

After the full run above, two things changed: the gateway default went from **3 to 4 replicas** (a ×1 midnight peak
needed ~2.5 cores of edge on this hardware — with 3 replicas the hardened baseline shed traffic at the spike), and the
Mission Control observability additions landed (journey sampling in the load generator, edge/store/stream metrics in
the control-plane summary — no change to any lab code path). The six labs whose outcome depends on edge capacity were
re-verified on the new default:

|---|---|---:|---:|---|

| [gc-pauses](labs/gc-pauses.md) | home p99 (ms, customer) | 510 | 20 | ✅ pass |

**A noisy-neighbour lesson.** The first attempt at this re-verification ran while an unrelated project on the same
machine pushed the load average to 28–49 on 16 cores. Four labs "failed" — the hardened gateway showed 10-second p99s
with no chaos applied — and none of it was BreakCart's fault: per-replica load was balanced and the shedder's admission
count matched the real in-flight count exactly. The verifier now samples the host load average during every run and
reports a non-passing lab as *inconclusive* when the host was saturated, instead of failing it. Two labs came back
inconclusive once more when the other workload resumed, and passed once it had stopped (host load still peaked at 14–19
during the passing runs), as shown above.

### Re-verification after making checkout resumable

Mission Control's business-invariant panel later caught **duplicate orders under the hardened baseline** during a
peak where the orders database pool was exhausted: a checkout created its order and reserved stock, then timed out on
its final read and answered 503, which (correctly) released the idempotency key — and the customer's retry created a
second order. Checkout is now resumable (order id derived from customer + key; idempotent reservation and coupon steps;
abandoned half-finished checkouts swept after 2 minutes). The checkout-sensitive labs were re-verified:

### Re-verification after the stock-commit retry and the longer payment hold

The retry-storm lab showed "payment captured but stock commit failed — needs manual reconciliation" while every
business invariant read 0: nothing retried the commit, so the hold could expire and the unit be sold twice. Failed
commits now go to a retry queue (`bc:commit-retry`) that the reconciler drains, a hold that expired first raises a
critical alarm, and the auditor has an eleventh invariant, `paid_uncommitted`. Minutes after it shipped, that invariant
caught the reconciler's own commit call swallowing errors during a heavily loaded oversell run (14 paid orders, all
settled by the reconciler, twelve of them about four seconds after their 64-second pre-charge holds had run out). The
reconciler now uses the same queue, and the pre-charge hold is PSP timeout + 2 minutes (was + 1). The labs on the
payment and commit paths were re-verified:

| Lab | Signal | Broken | Fixed | Result |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| [oversell-race](labs/oversell-race.md) | oversold units | 34 | 0 | ✅ pass |
| [phantom-sellout](labs/phantom-sellout.md) | phantom held units | 235 | 0 | ✅ pass |
| [retry-storm](labs/retry-storm.md) | service-to-service retries | 16,021 | 0 | ✅ pass |
| [double-charge](labs/double-charge.md) | duplicate charges + duplicate orders | 760 | 0 | ✅ pass |
| [payment-limbo](labs/payment-limbo.md) | orphan charges | 780 | 0 | ✅ pass |

`paid_uncommitted` (read from Prometheus) stayed at 0 in both runs of oversell-race, phantom-sellout, double-charge and
payment-limbo (whose fixed run has the reconciler settle hundreds of payments), and in retry-storm's broken run, whose
four immediate attempts got nearly every commit through. It did **not** stay at 0 in retry-storm's fixed run: budgeted
retries (correctly) don't retry the injected 500s, so about 30% of commits went to the queue, which retried them one at
a time against an inventory taking 150–250 ms per call. Up to 257 paid orders waited more than 30 s for their stock,
and IntegrityPaidUncommitted fired in the hardened run. Host load during these runs peaked between 7 and 18 (1-minute
load average, 16 cores).

### Re-verification after fixing the commit-retry queue

The queue now retries ten commits at a time, and before raising the critical alarm it checks that the order still
exists: a lab's data reset wipes orders whose retries are still queued, which would otherwise read as "sold twice".

| Lab | Signal | Broken | Fixed | Result |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| [retry-storm](labs/retry-storm.md) | service-to-service retries | 21,774 | 2,317 | ✅ pass |
| [payment-limbo](labs/payment-limbo.md) | orphan charges | 797 | 0 | ✅ pass |

`paid_uncommitted` stayed at 0 through all four runs; in retry-storm's fixed run the queue committed the stock of about
460 orders. None of that run's 2,317 retries went to inventory: they were timeouts on calls to catalog and orders while
the host's load peaked at 44.
